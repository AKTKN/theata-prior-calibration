# Statistical model and experimental protocol

## 1. Circuit, cooling threshold, and physical noise

### LIZ predictive trigger

The default LPT preset follows THeatA Sections V-C and V-D on rotated Z
memory at distances 3, 5 and 7 with three rounds. The pinned upstream cooling
scan compiles each threshold independently using one reused ancilla, a single
LIZ of capacity four, `greedy_alt` placement and alternating forward/reverse
rounds. Ion order carries between rounds and each round starts with zero
phonons. The Mainz profile supplies heating and timings. [THeatA Figure 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.30676v1#S5.SS4)

The archived `resident` arm uses planner v1, two resident coolant ions 9000
and 9001, `liz_pt_park: temp`, and `liz_pt_target_transversal: null` so the
trigger is also the target. It uses a maximum of 32 cycles, recools after
each ancilla measurement, and uses 1,000 μs for all cooling durations and
zero residual occupation in all three modes. The experiment uses the
requested threshold grid, which includes 1.0 in place of the archived
driver's 0.35. [Archived N32 sweep driver](https://github.com/MSTakahashiPJ/theata/blob/e06ab0365eaafe709d90582c44a50e537c46f396/experiments/run_lpt_theta_n32_1028.ps1)

The reconstructed CX circuit uses the thermal surface Approach B injector
and lockstep per-ion idle dephasing. The lookup takes each compiled MS
gate's hotter participant's first transverse occupation and uses the
upstream rounding and range bounds. It reads the full 15 probabilities
at unit scaling, with no virtual cap. The per-condition trace and metrics
record LUT clipping, the summed cooling time and the scheduled wall time.
The fixed noise values are $p_R=0.0001$, $p_{\rm meas}=0.0032$,
$p_{\rm reset}=0.0008$, and $\gamma_\phi=3\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$.

Heldout data come from the physical circuit. Full-detector MWPM uses the
decomposed DEM and produces the paper comparison in
`full_detector_metrics.csv`. Z-sector MWPM compares actual and estimated
priors on the same physical shots and records LER and prior RMSE in
`metrics.csv`. When projection has an unsupported event or ambiguous
logical signature, `model.json` records the reason and the full-detector
baseline is still evaluated. The ratio $p_L(d)/p_L(d+2)$ uses matching
threshold, decoder and calibration size. Estimated rates are averaged
across calibration repetitions before taking the ratio. Zero denominators
are stored as undefined. The figures use complete-experiment LER.

Table III gives full-detector reference LERs of 0.0039, 0.00097 and 0.00018
at threshold 0.2 for distances 3, 5 and 7. The smoke preset checks the
pipeline at d=3. [THeatA Table III](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.30676v1#S5.SS3)

### Standard Stim direct-cooling sweep

The input is generated by
`stim.Circuit.generated("surface_code:rotated_memory_z", distance=d, rounds=r)`
or its `rotated_memory_x` counterpart. The default is $r=d$ and
configuration uses $r=d\,\mathtt{rounds\_per\_distance}$.
The input generator is called without
noise. The adapter expands the standard circuit's Clifford gates into the
native rotation/MS gate set, compiles those operations using THeatA, and
preserves measurement order and every detector and observable definition.
It does not substitute THeatA's ancilla-reuse surface-code circuits.

This adapter deliberately orders the quantum operations conservatively. THeatA
determines transport operations, scheduling, per-ion idle gaps, and phonon
values for that ordered operation sequence. Consequently, its timing is the
timing of this documented bridge, rather than a claim of optimal QCCD
compilation. The ideal native decompositions are checked by Clifford tableau
equality. The source, compiled ideal circuit, noisy circuit, compiler metadata,
and full DEMs are saved separately.

For each $(d,r,\text{basis},\theta)$, where $\theta$ is the cooling threshold,
the bridge independently compiles the complete circuit. It supplies $\theta$
as `CompilerConfig.cool_threshold_transversal`. Immediately before a native
MS gate, THeatA compares the maximum transverse phonon occupation of the two
target ions to $\theta$. Above the threshold it inserts a `CoolOp` and sets
the LIZ chain to the configured residual occupation. The chain can include
ions carrying quantum information under the upstream model's direct-cooling
assumption.
The model treats the cooling duration and residual phonon occupation as
separate fixed inputs, with no dynamic relation between them. Both remain
fixed along with transport heating, background heating, and operation times.
A `null` threshold disables this pre-MS policy.

For the $G$ native MS gates in a compiled circuit, let $n_g$ be the maximum
occupation in the first transverse mode of the target ions at gate $g$,
recorded after any pre-gate cooling. Let $a_{g,P}$ be the THeatA lookup
probability for nonidentity two-qubit Pauli $P$ at that location. The circuit
uses all 15 $a_{g,P}$ in the native `PAULI_CHANNEL_2`. The default lookup input
is $n_g$ with `phonon_scale=1.0`. The measured physical MS error is

$$
\mathtt{mean\_ms\_error}=\frac1G\sum_{g=1}^G\sum_{P\ne II} a_{g,P}.
$$

Single-qubit gate, reset, and readout probabilities use their fixed configured
values. The dephasing rate $\gamma_\phi$ is also fixed. A scheduled idle
interval of duration $\Delta t$ seconds receives

$$
p_Z(\Delta t)=\frac{1-\exp(-2\gamma_\phi\Delta t)}2.
$$

THeatA records phonons on its serial planning clock. Idle gaps and total
circuit execution time use the parallel scheduler's clock. The sum of
individual `CoolOp` durations measures total cooling operation time and
can include overlapping operations. The per-condition `hardware.json`
records both time measures, resolved compiler and hardware parameters,
per-gate occupation and LUT selection, and idle and MS noise summaries.
`physical_metrics.csv` aggregates the summaries. With `strict_lut: true`,
above-range conditions retain their physical trace and rejection reason.
With `strict_lut: false`, the upstream lookup selects its boundary entry;
the raw phonons and per-condition counts retain the above-range information.
THeatA's compiler and lookup implementation are provided by the pinned
upstream revision in [reference 4](#references).

## 2. Full actual DEM and the identifiable estimation target

Suppose the compiled full DEM has $M$ detectors, $K$ logical observables, and
$E$ independent error instructions. Let

$$
H^{\rm full}=[h_1,\ldots,h_E]\in\mathbb F_2^{M\times E},
\qquad
B=[b_1,\ldots,b_E]\in\mathbb F_2^{K\times E}.
$$

Column $h_e$ specifies the detectors flipped by instruction $e$, and $b_e$
specifies its logical observable flips. For shot $n$, the occurrence vector is
$f^{(n)}\in\mathbb F_2^E$, with mutually independent components

$$
f_e^{(n)}\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(p_e^{\rm full}).
$$

The observed vectors are

$$
d^{(n)}=H^{\rm full}f^{(n)}\in\mathbb F_2^M,
\qquad
\ell^{(n)}=Bf^{(n)}\in\mathbb F_2^K.
$$

Products and sums in these two equations are over $\mathbb F_2$. Different
shots are independent. Stim's DEM syntax and independent-event semantics are
specified in [reference 2](#references).

The full surface-code DEM generally includes events with three or four
detectors, coupling the X and Z check sectors. Two-point covariance inversion
does not, in general, identify every such event separately. The package
therefore uses the detector sector of the chosen memory observable: Z-check
detectors for Z memory and X-check detectors for X memory. This selection is
part of the experimental definition and is applied to both decoders.

Let $I=(i_1,\ldots,i_m)$ be the selected original detector IDs and let
$R\in\mathbb F_2^{m\times M}$ be the corresponding row-selection matrix.
Then $x^{(n)}=Rd^{(n)}$ is the decoder's detector vector. Each original event
has projected detector signature $Rh_e$ and the original logical signature
$b_e$. Events with the same pair of signatures form a group $\mathcal G_g$.
Their aggregate occurrence is the parity

$$
u_g^{(n)}=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathcal G_g} f_e^{(n)},
\qquad
q_g=\Pr(u_g^{(n)}=1)
=\frac{1-\prod_{e\in\mathcal G_g}(1-2p_e^{\rm full})}{2}.
$$

Distinct groups contain disjoint original events, so the $u_g$ remain
independent. Marginalization and aggregation therefore preserve exactly the
joint distribution of selected detectors and logical observables of the input
independent DEM. Events with no selected detector and no logical action
integrate out.

The resulting graph must satisfy the following structural conditions:

- Every surviving signature contains one or two selected detectors.
- A detector signature has exactly one logical signature.
- No surviving event has a logical action with zero selected detectors.
- Every event probability is on the identifiable branch $0\le q_g<1/2$.

Residual hyperedges, logical-only events, or conflicting logical labels cause
an explicit error. In particular, a Stim separator `^` describes components
of one correlated error instruction. Its targets are XORed before projection;
the components are never assigned independent probabilities merely because a
decomposition was supplied.

The inference target is the vector $q\in[0,1/2)^{E'}$ of these $E'$ projected,
aggregated events. It is not the vector of all original $E$ instruction
probabilities. `actual_full.dem` preserves the complete source model;
`actual_sector.dem` and `actual_priors.npy` contain the oracle marginal used
for the comparison. `known_structure.json` contains detector signatures,
logical signatures, coordinates, and detector-ID mapping, without any prior.

## 3. Pair probabilities from detection events

Calibration uses a binary matrix $X\in\{0,1\}^{N\times m}$, with
$X_{ni}=x_i^{(n)}$. Logical observations are not estimator inputs. Empirical raw
moments are

$$
\widehat\mu_i=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N X_{ni},
\qquad
\widehat\mu_{ij}=\frac1N\sum_{n=1}^N X_{ni}X_{nj},
\qquad
\widehat C_{ij}=\widehat\mu_{ij}-\widehat\mu_i\widehat\mu_j.
$$

The empirical covariance here uses normalization $1/N$. No $N/(N-1)$
sample-covariance correction is applied: the estimator substitutes empirical
probabilities into the moment identity. Float64 matrix multiplication avoids
overflow or Boolean multiplication semantics for binary input arrays.

For the population moments, define

$$
\alpha_i=1-2\mu_i,
\qquad
\beta_{ij}=1-2\mu_i-2\mu_j+4\mu_{ij}.
$$

These are $\mathbb E[(-1)^{x_i}]$ and
$\mathbb E[(-1)^{x_i+x_j}]$, respectively. Independence of the graph's error
events gives

$$
\frac{\alpha_i\alpha_j}{\beta_{ij}}=(1-2q_{ij})^2,
$$

where $q_{ij}$ is the probability of the unique two-detector event joining
$i$ and $j$, or zero if that support is absent. Choosing the branch
$q_{ij}<1/2$ yields the exact inversion

$$
q_{ij}=\frac12\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_i\alpha_j}{\beta_{ij}}}\right).
$$

This is Eq. S11 of [reference 1](#references). The implementation uses its
algebraically equivalent rationalized form

$$
q_{ij}=
\frac{2C_{ij}}
{\beta_{ij}+\sqrt{\alpha_i\alpha_j\beta_{ij}}},
$$

which avoids subtracting two nearly equal numbers at small error rates. It
does not use the paper's first-order approximation. Hatted empirical moments
are substituted to obtain $\widehat q_{ij}$.

`p_ij.npy` stores the full $m\times m$ matrix of these raw estimates. Its
diagonal is set to zero as a plotting convention; single-detector means remain
available in `moments.npz`. Finite negative values are retained. Invalid
square-root domains are represented by NaN. Only pairs present in the known
topology are used as decoder edges. Empirical correlations at nonedges are
diagnostics and do not expand the assumed model.

## 4. General mechanism indices and finite-sample regularization

Index the known projected mechanisms by $e=1,\ldots,E'$. Let $q_e$ be the
prior of mechanism $e$ and $S_e$ its detector support. Set $H_{ie}=1$ when
$i\in S_e$. Independence of mechanism occurrences gives

$$
1-2\mu_i=\prod_{f:H_{if}=1}(1-2q_f).
$$

For any incident mechanism $e$, isolating its factor gives

$$
q_e=\frac12\left[
1-\frac{1-2\mu_i}
{\prod_{\substack{f\ne e\\H_{if}=1}}(1-2q_f)}
\right],
\qquad i\in S_e.
$$

For the implemented graphlike model, every support has size one or two,
and each support occurs at most once after aggregation. For $S_e=\{i,j\}$,
the prior is $q_e=p_{ij}$ from S11. The remaining $|S_e|=1$ priors follow
from the displayed equation because all other incident priors have already
been recovered. This is S14--S16 in general mechanism-index notation.

A unified formulation covers arbitrary known supports: define
$a_e=-\log(1-2q_e)$, restore the detector means on the pair matrix diagonal,
and apply $-\log(1-2p)$ entrywise to obtain $\Gamma$. Then, over the real
numbers,

$$
\Gamma=H\operatorname{diag}(a)H^\top
=\sum_e a_eh_eh_e^\top.
$$

Every mechanism contributes a known matrix $h_eh_e^\top$ with unknown scalar
$a_e$. General identification from these moments requires the corresponding
coefficient matrix to have full column rank for interior parameter values.
The complete derivation and identifiability conditions are given in
[General prior derivation](general_prior_derivation.md). The executable
estimator continues to use the graphlike elimination described above.

Only existing boundary edges are estimated. No boundary edge is added merely
to absorb a residual. The product is evaluated through sums of
$\log(1-2q_{ij})$ and `expm1` for numerical stability.

Finite data can give negative or invalid pair estimates, or pair estimates
whose predicted marginal exceeds the measured marginal and hence produces a
negative boundary estimate. The default policy makes the decoder prior
explicitly finite and physical:

$$
\widetilde q_g=\min(q_{\max},\max(q_{\min},\widehat q_g)),
\quad
q_{\min}=10^{-8},\quad q_{\max}=0.499999.
$$

Nonfinite estimates receive $q_{\min}$. Boundary inversion uses the
regularized known pair priors, followed by the same regularization of its raw
boundary result. Both constants are configurable and independent of the true
priors. This clipping is an additional finite-sample procedure, not part of
the exact population identities. It introduces bias, including a positive
bias for edges whose unconstrained estimate falls below the floor. The raw
pair matrix and raw per-edge estimates are retained so the effect can be
quantified. In the infinite-shot limit a fixed floor also leaves a bias if the
true prior lies below that floor.

`strict_estimation: true` rejects nonphysical known-edge estimates instead of
accepting this correction (up to numerical tolerance near zero). Regardless
of that option, an empirical marginal at or above $1/2$ raises an error: the
positive-bias inversion is not resolved by silently clipping such a marginal.
Known-edge and boundary correction counts, invalid-pair counts, and all
fitted-marginal residuals are saved in `diagnostics.json`. The comparison
against true priors is performed after fitting and cannot alter the fit.

## 5. Sampling source and the interpretation of the benchmark

The standard Stim sweep can use `sample_source: dem` to sample the compiled **full** actual DEM,
including its multi-detector events, then selects the memory-sector detector
columns. Observable flips are sampled from the same full model. Thus the
actual marginal priors are exactly the target probabilities under the stated
independent-event model. They are not used to generate fictitious labels for
individual mechanisms, and mechanism occurrence labels are not exposed to
the estimator.

`sample_source: circuit` samples the compiled stochastic Pauli circuit
directly and is the LPT protocol. THeatA's general `PAULI_CHANNEL_2` outcomes at a physical
gate are mutually exclusive. The conversion to an independent Stim DEM uses
`approximate_disjoint_errors=True`, whose meaning is documented in
[reference 3](#references). This conversion can change the distribution.
Consequently, in circuit-sampling mode the covariance model and the oracle
DEM can both differ from the sampled circuit. Any residual discrepancy then
includes this model mismatch as well as finite calibration error. The
metadata records the conversion option and sampling semantics. The exact
algebraic inversion in Section 3 does not eliminate a mismatch of the
generating model.

## 6. Calibration size, heldout evaluation, and MWPM

For each $(d,r,\text{basis},\theta)$ and independent calibration repetition $s$, the package
generates one training array of size $N_{\max}$. A requested training size $N$
uses its first $N$ rows. The resulting training-size curve therefore uses
nested data, reducing unnecessary resampling and making changes in $N$
interpretable for a fixed realization. Points at different $N$ are dependent.
Different calibration repetitions use distinct deterministic random streams.

One additional test set of $T=\mathtt{test\_shots}$ shots is generated from an
independent stream. Its detector vectors and true observable flips are shared
by the oracle decoder and every estimated decoder at that $(d,r,\text{basis},\theta)$. Training
observable labels may be stored but are never read by the estimator. Neither
the test syndromes nor test logical outcomes are used for calibration.

For an edge prior $q_g$, ordinary MWPM uses

$$
w_g=\log\frac{1-q_g}{q_g}.
$$

PyMatching receives the explicit independent graphlike DEM and computes
logical-observable predictions with `decode_batch`
([reference 5](#references)). Both comparisons use the same selected detector
sector and the same edge/logical structure. Only the priors differ. The
`actual` curve therefore denotes **oracle-prior MWPM on the memory-sector
marginal**. It is not a comparison to an optimal correlated decoder of the
entire X/Z hypergraph.

If $\widehat\ell^{(n)}$ is a decoder prediction, its logical failure indicator
and estimated rate are

$$
Y_n=\mathbf 1\{\widehat\ell^{(n)}\ne\ell^{(n)}\},
\qquad
\widehat P_L=\frac1T\sum_{n=1}^T Y_n.
$$

The event is any logical-observable mismatch; the standard memory circuit
has one observable. Rates are per complete memory experiment of $r$ rounds.
The notebook optionally displays the equivalent per-round probability
$1-(1-\widehat P_L)^{1/r}$ and applies the same transformation to both Wilson
interval endpoints.

## 7. Statistical uncertainty and paired comparisons

Each reported rate includes a two-sided Wilson score interval. With
$z=\Phi^{-1}((1+c)/2)$ for confidence level $c$, its center and radius are

$$
\mathrm{center}=\frac{\widehat P_L+z^2/(2T)}{1+z^2/T},
\qquad
\mathrm{radius}=\frac{z}{1+z^2/T}
\sqrt{\frac{\widehat P_L(1-\widehat P_L)}{T}+\frac{z^2}{4T^2}}.
$$

These are approximate binomial confidence intervals conditional on the fixed
fitted decoder, assuming independent test shots. They quantify test sampling
uncertainty; they do not incorporate uncertainty from drawing a different
calibration set. Multiple calibration repetitions provide separate fitted
decoders and expose that additional variation. Because those repetitions
share test data, their failure counts must not be pooled as $sT$ independent
binomial observations. Confidence intervals across training sizes, cooling
thresholds, and distances are pointwise rather than a simultaneous confidence band.

Zero observed failures is retained as a rate estimate of zero with a positive
upper confidence limit. It is not evidence of an exactly zero failure
probability. On a logarithmic rate axis, a zero-failure result should be
displayed as an upper limit rather than an arbitrary positive replacement
for its point estimate.

Paired decoder comparison also records $A$, the shots on which only the
estimated decoder fails, and $B$, the shots on which only the oracle decoder
fails. Their rate difference is

$$
\widehat\Delta=\frac{A-B}{T}.
$$

The paired confidence interval subtracts simultaneous Clopper–Pearson
intervals for the two discordant probabilities, with Bonferroni allocation of
the error probability. This conservative interval uses the shared test
realizations directly. Finite-shot rate estimates are not forced to put the
oracle curve below every estimated curve.
